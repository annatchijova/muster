Live on Monad testnet · chain id 10143

Buy incident-response capacity before the incident — not a retainer, a reservation.

Muster turns a specialist's standing commitment — "4 units of ZK-security incident response, usable Oct 1–31, 30-minute acknowledgment" — into a collateral-backed, transferable onchain position. A buyer reserves it; if the incident never comes, they resell it before it expires instead of eating the cost. If it comes, a collateral bond is what makes the SLA real instead of a promise.

01

Why this needed a smart contract, specifically

Not because the domain is "crypto" — because three properties a retainer, a Calendly link, or an NDA'd SLA document cannot give a buyer are exactly what a contract makes non-negotiable between two parties who don't trust each other.

Typical approach Muster
The same hour can informally be promised to two clients.
reserve() only succeeds once per position — enforced, not policed.
A missed SLA means a dispute, maybe a chargeback.
A collateral bond pays out automatically on a missed deadline.
An unused reservation is a sunk cost — a retainer bills monthly either way.
transfer() moves it to another buyer before it expires. Nothing paid, nothing lost.
Searching mid-incident means negotiating with whoever's free, under pressure.
Price and provider are locked in at reservation time, before urgency gives anyone leverage.

02

Founder & market readiness

The specific first user is not "crypto traders." Two named sides, not a generic market:

Supply side

The boutique specialist

An independent ZK-security auditor, a small DFIR shop, an incident-response contractor who already sells time this way informally — today either idle between engagements (a sunk cost) or locked into a retainer that pays whether or not they're used.

Demand side

The security/eng lead

At a mid-size Web3 protocol or DAO. Can't justify a full-time incident-response hire, and today has nothing better than a "call me if something happens" arrangement — no SLA, no collateral, no recourse if that person isn't free when it matters.

Not limited to security specialists — the same gap exists for any rare, spiky, hard-to-staff specialist (an electrician, a structural engineer, a notary). A pooled market — "4 units of anyone certified ELECTRICIAN_L2," not "Juan specifically" — is the same TermsClass shape already built and tested, unmodified.

04

Path forward & business model

Next step
Pilot with 2-3 real security boutiques/DAOs post-hackathon — real counterparties on testnet first, revenue mechanism second. A named, narrow first cohort, not a generic "we'll grow users" line.
New verticals
Data, not code — a TermsClass (domain, window, SLA, price, arbitration panel) is the only thing that defines a vertical. DFIR, pentest-on-call, legal/compliance response, or a non-security trade entirely: same contract, new config row.
Revenue model
Not built yet, stated honestly: no fee-capture exists today — every unit of value goes to provider or buyer. A basis-point fee at activate() or settle() was deliberately deferred as a monetization decision, not an architecture one.
Reputation routing
Tracked today (ProviderStatsUpdated, indexed), not yet acted on — activate()'s FIFO fill doesn't yet weight by reputation. Named as the honest line between "tracked" and "acted on," not overclaimed.

What this is not claiming: product-market fit today, paying customers, or validated demand beyond this hackathon's demonstration and the pilot plan above. The claim is narrower and checkable — the architecture doesn't need to be rebuilt to go from one vertical on testnet to many verticals on a fee-generating mainnet, because it was built fungible and multi-vertical from Level 2 onward, not as a single-provider prototype later outgrown.
